# EYD V rules for the Indonesian verse text

Source: *Ejaan Bahasa Indonesia yang Disempurnakan* (EYD) Edisi V, Keputusan Kepala Badan Pengembangan dan Pembinaan Bahasa No. 0424/I/BS.00.01/2022 (16 Agustus 2022). References are [BAB.letter.item]. Examples marked "e.g." are applications to Bible text, not quotations from the document.

## Capital letters
1. First letter of a sentence [I.F.1] and of a direct quotation [I.F.6]. A quotation interrupted by a speech tag continues in lowercase: "Besok pagi," kata Rino, "mereka akan berangkat."
2. Names of persons, including epithets [I.F.2].
3. Religion, holy books and God, including titles and pronouns for God and abbreviations of His names [I.F.7]: Allah, Tuhan, Alkitab; "Allah Yang Maha Kuasa akan menunjukkan jalan-Nya." "Ya, Tuhan, bimbinglah hamba ke jalan yang Engkau beri rahmat." So: Engkau, Dia/Ia, -Nya, -Mu, and Aku/Ku- when God speaks (applying I.F.7 and II.H.1); lowercase for people.
4. Names of peoples, tribes and languages [I.F.11]: bangsa Israel, suku Lewi (e.g.), bahasa Ibrani (e.g.). Not when derived: pengindonesiaan [I.F.12].
5. Names of months, days and festivals [I.F.13]: bulan Agustus, hari Jumat, hari Natal; e.g. hari Sabat, hari Paskah.
6. Geographic names [I.F.16]: Sungai Yordan, Gunung Sinai, Kota Yerusalem (e.g.). Lowercase when no name follows [I.F.17]: berlayar ke teluk, mandi di sungai, menyeberangi selat.
7. Titles and offices before a name or used as a name [I.F.8-10]: Perdana Menteri Nehru; e.g. Raja Daud, Imam Besar Kayafas. Titles used as address: "Selamat datang, Yang Mulia." Kinship words used as address or reference are capitalized: "Kapan Bapak berangkat?" With a possessive they stay lowercase: bapak dan ibu kita [I.F.23].

## Writing words
1. Affixes attach: berjalan, dijual, pembaca [II.B.1.a]. Bound form maha- is separate and capitalized for names or attributes of God: Yang Maha Esa, Tuhan Yang Maha Kuasa, Yang Maha Pengasih, Tuhan Yang Maha Pengampun [II.B.1.e].
2. Reduplication takes a hyphen: anak-anak, berjalan-jalan; of a compound, repeat the first element: kapal-kapal barang [II.B.2].
3. Compounds are separate: orang tua, rumah sakit; attached only when they take prefix and suffix together: dilipatgandakan, menyebarluaskan, pertanggungjawaban; separate with only one affix: bertepuk tangan, garis bawahi [II.B.3.a-d]. Written as one word: apalagi, bagaimana, barangkali, daripada, dukacita, sukacita, matahari, padahal, perilaku, sukarela, segitiga [II.B.3.e].
4. Prepositions di, ke, dari are separate: di dalam, ke luar kota, dari rumah [II.D]; the prefixes di-, ke- attach: dijual.
5. Particles -lah, -kah, -tah attach: Bacalah, Apakah. Pun is separate: Apa pun. It attaches in these conjunctions: adapun, andaipun, ataupun, bagaimanapun, biarpun, jikapun, kalaupun, kendatipun, maupun, meskipun, sekalipun, sementangpun, sungguhpun, walaupun. Per is separate: satu per satu, per meter [II.E].
6. ku- and kau- attach to the next word: kujual, kaubaca; -ku, -mu, -nya attach to the previous word: bukunya. A free kau is separate: Aku ingin kau bersungguh-sungguh [II.H]. Between a lowercase word and a capital element use a hyphen: ciptaan-Nya, jalan-Nya, hamba-Mu (e.g.) [III.E.5, I.F.7].
7. si and sang are separate: si pengirim, sang suami. Sang is capitalized when part of God's name: Sang Pencipta [II.I].
8. Established loanwords keep their spelling [IV.B.4]: faedah, heran, kabar, khotbah, majelis, napas, paham, perlu, pikir, sahabat, sehat, setan, soal, syahadat, terjemah; Senin, Selasa, Rabu, Kamis.

## Numbers
1. A number expressible in one word is spelled out in running text: tiga kali, seribu buku; but use digits in enumerations: 50 bus, 100 minibus, 250 sedan [II.G.2].
2. Digits for measures (length, weight, area, volume, time) and values (money, percent): 5 kilogram, 2 tahun 6 bulan 5 hari, 1 jam 20 menit, Rp5.000,00, 5%, 7 persen [II.G.3]; so e.g. 3 hari, 40 hari.
3. A number of more than one word does not begin a sentence in digits: begin with Sebanyak or reword [II.G.4]. Large numbers may be part words: 500 ribu, 90 juta rupiah [II.G.5].
4. Ordinals: abad ke-7 or abad ketujuh, both allowed [II.G.9]; this project uses the word form (hari ketiga). Fractions in words: setengah, tiga perempat [II.G.8.b].
5. Thousands separator is a period: 13.000 pulau; decimal is a comma: 12,5 m; no period in years: tahun 1998 [III.A.8-9, III.B.12].

## Punctuation
1. Comma: between items in a series, with dan before the last [III.B.1]; before tetapi, melainkan, sedangkan [III.B.2]; after a clause that comes before the main clause (Kalau diundang, saya akan datang) but not when it follows (Saya akan datang kalau diundang) [III.B.3-4]; after sentence connectives such as Oleh karena itu, Jadi, Dengan demikian [III.B.5]; around interjections and vocatives: "Ya, Tuhan, ...", "Nak, ..." [III.B.6]; around an apposition: Soekarno, Presiden I Republik Indonesia, merupakan ... ; none around a restrictive clause: Siswa yang lulus dengan nilai tinggi akan diterima [III.B.13].
2. Direct speech takes double quotation marks. Introduced speech: Kata nenek saya, "Kita harus berbagi dalam hidup ini." Speech first: "Kita harus berbagi dalam hidup ini," kata nenek saya, "karena manusia adalah makhluk sosial." No comma when the quote ends in ? or !: "Di mana Saudara tinggal?" tanya Pak Lurah [III.B.7-8, III.J.1]. The final period of a quoted sentence stands inside the closing mark (see the example for Pasal 31 in III.J.1). A quotation inside a quotation takes single marks [III.K.1]. Single marks also give meanings: tergugat 'yang digugat' [III.K.2].
3. Semicolon between coordinate clauses in place of a conjunction (Kerbau melenguh; kambing mengembik; kuda meringkik.) and between list parts that already contain commas [III.C].
4. Colon after a complete statement that directly introduces a list or explanation, not when the list is part of the sentence [III.D.1-2].
5. Hyphen: reduplication; between capital and lowercase or letters and digits: se-Indonesia, ke-2, ciptaan-Nya; paired elements: suami-istri [III.E.2, 5, 9]. Dash for an inserted remark and for 'sampai' between numbers or places: tahun 2019—2022 [III.F].
6. Exclamation mark for strong emotion, summons or command: Bayarlah pajak tepat waktu! [III.H]. Ellipsis for interrupted or trailing speech; at the end of a sentence it is followed by the final mark (…. or …?) [III.I].
7. Parentheses for extra information [III.L].

## Project conventions (not from EYD)
Plain text only (no italics or bold). Proper names keep the form given in the glosses. Do not use square brackets or slashes to join alternatives, and no apostrophe contractions ('kan), which EYD calls non-standard [III.O].
